VFS Purdue

As President of Vertical Flight Systems Purdue, I lead a 30-member interdisciplinary team developing an autonomy-enabled, human-carrying eVTOL for the GoAERO Competition. Our team earned the U.S. University Innovation Award and raised more than $74,000 toward the aircraft's development.

250kg Vehicle

Our full-scale aircraft is a human-carrying emergency-response eVTOL designed for the final stage of the GoAERO Competition. I coordinate aircraft-level technical and logistical decisions while focusing on the 100 V monitoring and balancing BMS for its 14.4 kWh battery pack.

40% Scale Prototype

This 32 kg demonstrator validated the team's propulsion, avionics, and autonomy architecture before committing to the full-scale aircraft. I led avionics and propulsion integration and helped direct the flight-test campaign from initial takeoff through fully autonomous missions.

L.I.D.A.R. Drone

L.I.D.A.R. (Light-based Indoor Drone for Autonomous Recognition) is a custom 3D-printed, five-inch multirotor I designed and manufactured for autonomous indoor flight. It uses a Raspberry Pi and a custom LiDAR-based SLAM algorithm written in C++ to map its surroundings, estimate its position, and navigate indoors without collisions.

Project in progress

Modular Control ESC

This project is a modular motor-driver platform for deploying and comparing motor-control algorithms and rotor-position estimators on the same hardware. The planned design uses an STM32H743, a three-phase inverter, gate drivers, current sensing, and DC-link measurement so algorithms such as FOC, deadbeat control, MPC, and DTC can be evaluated under comparable conditions. I'm currently designing the hardware to enable multiple control algorithms to be tested on the same inverter and motor, with the goal of providing a flexible platform for research and development in motor control.

  • Position feedback: support for encoders, Hall sensors, and sensorless observers.
  • Power target: 1 kW
